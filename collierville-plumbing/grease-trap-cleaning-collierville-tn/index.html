    Grease Trap Cleaning Cost in Collierville, TN

    Cost follows trap size and fill level. Small under-sink traps go quickly. A 1,500-gallon interceptor left five months needs a truck, a pressure wand and disposal fees by the gallon, which puts it at the top of the range. A steady schedule lowers what each visit costs.

    Grease Line Jetting in Collierville

    Grease that slips past a full trap coats the 4-inch line out to the main. It builds up in rings, thickest at the bottom of the pipe. Snaking just bores a small path that closes up in weeks, but a rotating jetter nozzle strips grease off the full wall of the pipe. A camera confirms it afterward.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Cleaning in Collierville

    Gravity interceptors in the parking lot often hold 750 to 2,000 gallons across two or three compartments. Each compartment gets pumped separately. On big tanks we break the grease cap with a pressure wand first, since a cap that has set up hard will plug the suction hose and leave half the grease behind. No one goes inside without confined-space gear.

    Commercial Grease Trap Cleaning in Collierville

    Restaurants and hospital kitchens in Collierville run the same basic setup, a trap or interceptor sitting between the sinks and the sewer, sized by flow rate or by gallons. We pump it empty and scrape it. Before the lid goes back on, we look over both tees and the baffle wall for cracks or missing pieces. Most kitchens land between every two weeks and every three months.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Maintenance in Collierville, TN

    Under-sink hydromechanical traps are rated by flow, usually 10 to 50 gallons per minute, and they hold far less than people expect. In a busy kitchen, that means cleaning every week or two. We take the lid off, remove grease and solids, scrape the baffles, check the flow control fitting on the inlet, and replace the lid gasket if it is flattened or torn. Most odor from these units comes from a bad gasket.

    Compliance and Documentation in Collierville

    Pretreatment rules in Tennessee are usually set and enforced at the city or sewer district level, and nearly all of them want proof of pumping kept on site. Every visit ends with a dated record. The record shows gallons removed, grease and solids depth before pumping, baffle and tee condition, and which hauler took the load. A binder by the office door works fine.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance Plans in Collierville

    Most pretreatment programs use the 25 percent rule: pump before floating grease and settled solids together take up a quarter of the trap's liquid depth. Some cities set a fixed interval instead, often 90 days for interceptors. Whichever rule your program uses, we schedule to it. Changes are easy.
