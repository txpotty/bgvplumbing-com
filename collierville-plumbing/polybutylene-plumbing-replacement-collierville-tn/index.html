    Frequently Asked Questions About Polybutylene Plumbing Replacement in Collierville

    PEX replacements usually run $4,000 to $10,000, and copper often runs $8,000 to $15,000 or more. Access changes the price. Size, fixtures and attic or crawlspace access set most of the cost, and you get a written quote after we see the house.

    Replacing a whole supply system takes special tools, code knowledge and real repipe experience. It takes skill and time. DIY mistakes can leave leaks hidden in walls, fail inspection or void the pipe warranty, which is why we recommend leaving it to licensed plumbers.

    Replacing an entire supply system requires routing, pressure testing and permits. Licensed crews work faster and leave you warranties and paperwork.

    Chlorine and chloramine in treated water break down the plastic from the inside, making it brittle. Hot lines and fittings usually go before the rest.

    Gray or blue plastic pipe stamped PB2110 is the main sign. Cracked fittings are a warning. Since poly can look normal right before it splits, a home from roughly 1978 to 1995 with this pipe should plan a replacement even before any leaks.

    Every poly replacement we do comes with a written warranty on materials and labor. PEX makers add their own. Both warranties come in writing with the photos and permit record, all in one place for later.

    Both are good choices. PEX is cheaper. PEX is flexible, handles freezing and movement better and installs faster, while copper is rigid, tolerates heat and sunlight and has a long track record, so the right answer depends on budget, water and preference.

    Properly installed PEX usually lasts 25 to 50 years. Copper often goes further. In water that does not attack it, Type L copper can last 50 years or more, and both are major upgrades over poly.
