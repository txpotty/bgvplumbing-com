    Frequently Asked Questions About New Construction Plumbing in Johnson City

    Most new single-family homes in the Johnson City area cost between $12,000 and $25,000 to plumb, with larger custom homes running higher. A bathroom adds a few thousand on its own. Tankless heaters and gas runs add to it. Our bid is fixed per phase, so the number you sign is the number you pay unless the plans change.

    Expect plumbing to touch the schedule three times. The under-slab work runs one to three days. Top-out runs three to five days, then trim needs one or two days after cabinets and tile are done. Inspections add a day each.

    Karst ground with sinkholes is common in parts of Tennessee, and shifting soil above that rock can crack buried pipes over time. Soil movement works on any pipe buried under concrete. Our crews bed drains in sand or gravel, sleeve pipes through the slab and run PEX water services where permitted. Sleeves are cheap.

    The term is written into your contract before work starts. It covers our labor and the fittings we supply. Fixtures, water heaters and PEX systems carry their own manufacturer warranties, and we register water heaters in the owner's name at final so the claim process is simple later.

    PEX handles most water supply in new homes. It bends around corners and needs far fewer fittings inside walls than copper does. Drains and vents are usually Schedule 40 PVC or ABS, depending on what the local code allows and what the plans specify for the building. We still use copper near water heaters.

    We install Moen, Kohler, Delta and American Standard, plus most other major brands a builder or designer specifies. Homeowner-supplied fixtures are fine. Have them delivered before trim so we can open each box, since a missing part or wrong finish can delay trim a week.

    We handle the plumbing permit work and inspections on our scope. The general contractor normally holds the main permit. We book every plumbing inspection, meet the inspector, and clear corrections before the next trade needs the wall.
