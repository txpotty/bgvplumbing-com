    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Trap Installation in Kingsport, TN

    Hydromechanical traps use baffles and a vented flow control to pull grease out of water moving at a set rate, usually 20 to 100 gallons per minute. They fit indoors, under a sink or in a floor pit. The flow control is not optional. Without it, a dumped sink pushes water through faster than the unit is rated for and the grease goes out the outlet. We look for units certified to PDI G-101 or ASME A112.14.3.

    Grease Trap Sizing and Placement in Kingsport, TN

    Sizing starts with fixture drain rates and ends with where the trap can physically go. A trap 40 feet from the sink lets grease cool and stick in the pipe before it ever reaches the unit. Closer is better. We also check that a pump truck can reach an outdoor tank with about 100 feet of hose, since a tank the hauler cannot reach costs you more on every service.

    Replacement and Upgrade Services in Kingsport, TN

    Steel traps from decades back rust through at the baffle seams, and by then the outlet is usually passing grease straight to the sewer. A camera down the outlet line tells us how much damage the old unit already did. Sometimes the line needs jetting too. Upgrading also gives you a chance to move from a 20 GPM trap to a 35 or 50 if the menu or seat count grew.

    Gravity Grease Trap Installation in Kingsport

    A gravity interceptor works on time, not machinery. Wastewater slows down in the tank long enough for grease to float and solids to settle. We size by fixture count and retention time, then match the tank to what the local pretreatment office for your building will accept. No moving parts to fail.

    Automatic Grease Removal Units in Kingsport

    An automatic grease removal unit heats the trapped grease and skims it into a container on a timer, so staff empty a bucket instead of scraping a tank. They cost more up front. Plan for a dedicated circuit near the unit, which your electrician will need to run. Fry-heavy kitchens like them most.

    New Restaurant Build-Out Grease Traps in Kingsport

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. We work from the architect's plumbing sheets and the kitchen equipment schedule. Changes get expensive. Moving a trap after the concrete cures means cutting the floor, so we catch problems while it is still paper.

    Hydromechanical Grease Trap Installation in Kingsport

    Flow rating is the number that matters on a hydromechanical trap, and it has to match the fastest-draining fixture combination in your kitchen. A 50 GPM trap on a line that sees 75 GPM will pass grease every lunch rush. We do the math on paper. The sink volume and drain-down time give the GPM number, and we show you the calculation.

    Gravity Grease Interceptor Installation in Kingsport, TN

    Concrete or polyethylene, a buried gravity interceptor is a two-compartment tank with tees on the inlet and outlet that reach down below the grease layer. Precast concrete weighs more and handles a parking lot better than plastic. Poly is easier to set. Plastic tanks in wet soil need hold-down straps or a concrete collar so they do not float when pumped.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Kingsport

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. Most of the difference is labor, mainly digging and concrete repair. Permits add a little. You get a written, itemized number after we see the kitchen.

    Almost always, yes. You will usually need a plumbing permit plus a sign-off from the sewer authority's pretreatment program. We pull the permit, submit the sizing calculation and schedule the rough and final inspections. Expect an inspector on site.

    Nearly every food business needs one. Plumbing codes require a grease interceptor wherever fixtures discharge grease-laden waste, which covers restaurants, bakeries and most food prep spaces. Some coffee shops and prepackaged food counters get a waiver, but that decision belongs to the local pretreatment office, not the landlord or the previous tenant. Ask before you sign the lease.

    Plastic hydromechanical traps often last 20 years or more, while coated steel traps can rust out in 7 to 15 depending on how harsh the wastewater runs. A precast interceptor can last a very long time if it is pumped on schedule and the coating holds. Cleaning habits matter most. A trap that is scraped with a metal tool every week or left full of grease for months ages much faster than one that gets regular service.

    Coverage comes from two places. The manufacturer warrants the trap body and lid, and the term depends on the brand and whether it is steel or plastic. We put our labor warranty in writing on the invoice, covering leaks at the joints we made and any problem with how we set and leveled the unit. Damage from skipped cleanings falls outside it.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. Busy kitchens with small indoor traps often hit that mark every week. Outdoor tanks go longer. Many gravity interceptors are pumped every 30 to 90 days, and your local FOG program may set a maximum interval no matter what the trap looks like.

    Watch the pump-out bills. Rising service frequency means the trap has been outgrown or the baffles have failed. Smell is another clue. Slow drains near the trap and failed inspections point to replacement too.

    An under-sink hydromechanical trap with good access is usually a half-day job, including the flow control and a leak test. Recessing one into the slab adds a day or two for saw-cutting and patching. Outdoor tanks are different. A buried interceptor runs two to five working days after permit, mostly driven by the dig and the paving repair.
