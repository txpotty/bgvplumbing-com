    We put a labor warranty in writing on every replacement. Pipe manufacturers cover the material separately. If a fitting we made ever leaks, we come back and fix it at no charge, and the terms are on the estimate before you sign anything.

    Copper laid in decent soil commonly lasts 50 years and often longer. PEX makers back it for decades. The things that shorten either one, in our experience digging up failed lines, are aggressive soil chemistry, sharp rock against the pipe, and poor bedding. Good install, long life.

    Type K copper, PEX-a, and HDPE for trenchless pulls. Each one is listed for potable water underground. The pick depends on run length, the soil in your yard, the water pressure at the street and your budget, and on a long run PEX usually wins. Copper costs more.

    Hot, humid summers and cool winters with frequent freeze and thaw cycles are the main climate stresses on plumbing in Tennessee. We set new service lines at or below the frost depth local code requires, protect the exposed section at the meter, and use pipe that tolerates the swings, which in cold country often means PEX.

    Start with the meter. Close the main shutoff at the house and watch the dial. A moving dial with the main closed means the leak is outside. If it stops, the leak is inside, often a running toilet or a slab leak, and that is a different repair.
