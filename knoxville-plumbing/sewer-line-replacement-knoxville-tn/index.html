    Sewer Pipe Materials We Install in Knoxville, TN

    PVC covers most of our trenched work. We lay SDR 35 PVC for most buried runs, Schedule 40 where a thicker wall is called for, and fused HDPE for pipe bursting. Crawlspaces are the most common foundation here, though basements are more common in hilly areas where homes are built into slopes. Cast iron still has a place inside some buildings.     Replacing a Collapsed Sewer by Trench in Knoxville

    Collapsed pipe cannot be burst or lined. When the camera stops at a pile of broken clay or a flattened section of Orangeburg, the only fix is to dig down, clear the debris and lay new pipe through that stretch. Digging also lets us see the soil around the break. Sometimes we find a washout or a root mat that explains why the pipe failed in the first place.

    Upgrading Old Sewer Pipe Materials in Knoxville

    Clay, cast iron and Orangeburg all fail in their own ways. Roots get into clay joints, cast iron rusts out along the invert, and Orangeburg fiber pipe squashes flat over the years. PVC and HDPE do none of that. Fused HDPE has no joints at all, so roots have nowhere to start.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Knoxville

    A collapsed run or a long belly usually means we dig. The crew trenches the full length of the lateral, removes the failed pipe and sets new PVC on compacted bedding with steady fall. Where we can, the sod is cut and set aside before the digging starts. The inspector sees open pipe. Backfill goes in compacted lifts so the trench line does not sink into a ditch a year later.

    Cured-in-Place Sewer Lining in Knoxville

    Lining builds a new pipe inside the old one. We clean the line first, often with a cutter and a jetter, then pull a resin-saturated felt tube into place and inflate it so it cures hard against the host pipe. Collapsed pipe will not work. It is a strong choice under slabs and finished rooms. We say plainly whether your line qualifies.
