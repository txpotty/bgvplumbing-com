    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Knoxville

    Standard drain snaking in Knoxville, TN usually costs $150 to $500 depending on where the clog sits and how easy it is to reach. Mains with heavy roots, or jobs that need jetting, run $400 to $1,000 and sometimes more. Camera work is extra. You get a firm price after the tech looks at the drain and before any cable goes in.

    A limited guarantee for a set number of days comes with most rooter work. If the line clogs again within that time, we return at no extra charge.

    Rental machines are easy to get, but they are dangerous in untrained hands. A cable can kink and wrap a hand or wrist fast. They also get stuck. Getting a rental cable stuck in clay pipe can mean digging it out, which costs far more than a rooter visit.

    Clearing a clog in Knoxville does not need a permit. Repair work is different. A new cleanout, a dig to fix broken pipe or a sewer replacement usually needs a plumbing permit. We handle the permit and the inspection.

    Snaking runs a spinning cable with a cutting head through the clog. A jetter uses water at thousands of psi to scour the inside of the pipe from wall to wall. Jetting leaves the pipe cleaner. We usually jet recurring grease lines and scaled mains, and snake the one-off clogs.

    Chemicals sometimes open a small hole in a hair clog, but they will not touch roots or a grease plug. They can soften old PVC glue joints and pit cast iron over time. They also burn skin. A cable takes the clog out instead of melting a narrow path through it, which keeps the drain open longer.

    Chemical drain openers rarely clear a real clog and can eat at old pipe. Old cast iron and the thin brass traps found in Knoxville's older homes suffer the worst of it. They also put our techs at risk. Standing water full of caustic chemicals splashes when we open the trap, so tell us if you poured anything in before we arrived.

    Plan on 1 to 3 hours for a single clogged drain. Heavy main line root cutting or a long jetting run can take 3 to 6 hours. You get a time estimate first. After looking at the cleanout and hearing which drains are slow, the tech can narrow the estimate.
