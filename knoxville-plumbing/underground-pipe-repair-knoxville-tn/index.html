    Frequently Asked Questions About Underground Pipe Repair in Knoxville

    Simple buried pipe repairs usually run $500 to $2,500. Sewer replacements cost more. Extensive trenchless sewer replacements can be $5,000 to $15,000 or more, depending on where the pipe runs, how bad the damage is and the method used to fix it. The price comes after we look.

    Most point repairs take a few hours to a day. Replacements take longer. A full service line replacement typically takes one to two days, with footage and method setting the pace. The schedule comes with the quote.

    Check the meter with every fixture off. Watch the dial. If it moves, water is going somewhere, and a wet or sinking spot in the yard or a hissing sound near the meter box usually points to the buried service. A locate visit settles it.

    It is pipe repair or replacement through small access pits. We do it regularly. Pipe bursting and cured-in-place lining are the two main methods we use for Knoxville homeowners, and both leave most of the yard, driveway and landscaping undisturbed compared to an open trench. We check yours first.

    Look for wet ground that never dries. Pressure drops matter too. Unexplained bill increases, foul odors near the cleanout or gurgling drains point to trouble in the pipe you cannot see. A locate visit confirms it.

    For water lines we use PEX or Type K copper. Both are rated for burial. Sewer and drain work uses Schedule 40 PVC, and trenchless jobs use fused HDPE, and every one of these holds up to soil and water far better than galvanized or clay. Local code decides the final pick.

    Less digging is the big one. Your lawn survives. Trenchless methods leave driveways, patios and established landscaping alone, and the savings on putting the yard back often cover the higher method price. It is often faster.

    Acoustic ground microphones pick up the sound of escaping water. We also use tracer gas. Hydrogen-nitrogen gas pushed into the line rises through the soil at the leak, while a locator shows the pipe path for the survey. Together they narrow it to a few feet.
