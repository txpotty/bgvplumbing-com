    Jet Pump System Repair in Knoxville, TN

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up through a suction line with an ejector. Air leaks on the suction side cause most failures. BGV Plumbing checks the foot valve, the ejector nozzle and every suction fitting, then reprimes the pump and watches it reach cut-out pressure. Nonstop running without pressure usually means lost prime or an air leak.

    Jet Pump Repair in Knoxville, TN

    Shallow wells and booster systems often use jet pumps. Lost prime, seal leaks and impeller wear account for most jet pump calls. Our crew in Knoxville restores prime, replaces worn parts and checks the suction line for air leaks. Where water levels swing through the year, a falling level can starve the pump and cost it prime.

    Pressure Tank Repair in Knoxville

    Short cycling is the classic pressure tank symptom, with the pump clicking on and off every few seconds while a faucet runs. Usually the bladder has failed or the tank has lost its air charge. Precharge gets checked on an empty tank. It should sit about 2 psi below the cut-in pressure, so a 40/60 switch wants roughly 38 psi in the tank.

    Submersible Pump Pulling and Repair in Knoxville

    Sand wears impellers down over time, and a pump that once filled the tank in a minute starts taking three. Other common failures are the motor, the splice and the check valve. We test the motor and wire from the surface before deciding whether to pull. Replacement pumps get sized to the yield and depth of the well.

    Well Pump Diagnostic Services in Knoxville, TN

    Voltage, amperage, pressure and flow rate tell us most of what is happening down the well without pulling a single length of pipe. High amp draw points to a weak motor. Low flow with normal amp draw points to a plugged intake or a falling water level, which is a different repair. Testing first saves a pull.

    Control Box & Electrical Repair in Knoxville, TN

    Capacitors, relays and corroded terminals inside the control box cause many pump failures. A tripped breaker is often the first sign. With the power locked out, we test and replace the failed parts and tighten every terminal. Wiring meets Tennessee code when the job is done.
