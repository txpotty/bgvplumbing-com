    Types of Toilet Replacements in Knoxville, TN

    Comfort-Height Toilet Replacement in Knoxville

    Going from a standard bowl to a comfort-height model raises the seat two or three inches, which matters a lot for seniors and anyone recovering from knee or hip surgery. The drain location stays the same. If there is room, a grab bar beside it helps too, and we can anchor that into a stud during the same visit.

    Toilet Flange Repair During Replacement in Knoxville

    Pulling the old toilet is the only chance to see the flange, and a cracked plastic ring or a rusted cast iron flange will ruin a new install within a year. We check it every time. A split ring gets a stainless repair ring, while a flange that sits below the finished floor gets a spacer or extender so the seal compresses properly.

    Wall-Hung Toilet Replacement in Knoxville

    Wall-hung toilets mount to a steel carrier inside the wall, so replacing one means checking the carrier bolts, the gasket on the outlet and the condition of the wall finish around it. The carrier itself sometimes needs new bolts or a coupling. We match the new bowl to the existing carrier whenever possible, which avoids opening the wall.

    Commercial Toilet Replacement in Knoxville, TN

    Office and retail restrooms wear out toilets faster than any house, and a replacement there usually means a floor-mount or wall-hung bowl with a flushometer instead of a tank. Work gets scheduled around your business hours. We match the bowl to the gallons per flush on the valve, since a mismatch leaves waste behind or wastes water.

    Cracked Toilet Replacement in Knoxville

    Porcelain cracks below the waterline are not safely repairable, and epoxy fixes on a tank under constant water pressure rarely last more than a few months. You should replace it. The cracked unit comes out, and we look at the floor under it before setting the new one, since a slow crack leak often soaks the subfloor first.

    ADA and Commercial Toilet Replacement in Knoxville, TN

    Public restrooms need bowls rated for heavy use, and many need to meet ADA height and clearance rules when a fixture is replaced. Clearances get checked before we order anything. Sensor flush valves are an easy add during the swap, and they cut down on unflushed bowls.

    Toilet Brands We Replace in Knoxville

    Brand matters less than model, because every major maker sells a great toilet and a mediocre one at nearly the same price. We look at the flush valve size and MaP score. Parts availability counts too. A toilet with proprietary parts can turn a simple repair into a special order later.

    Comfort Height Toilet Installation in Knoxville

    Chair height toilets put the seat about 17 to 19 inches off the floor, roughly two inches higher than a standard bowl, and that makes a real difference for knees and hips. The drain stays the same. Many people pair it with an elongated bowl and a soft-close seat, and the swap takes the same couple of hours as any other.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Toilet Replacement in Knoxville

    Expect roughly $350 to $800 for a standard, chair height or high-efficiency toilet installed, with the old one removed and hauled away. A rusted flange or a cramped bathroom can raise it. You get a firm number up front.

    A pro install costs more up front, but it comes with a flange check, the right closet bolts and a warranty if the seal leaks later. A DIY seal that leaks can quietly rot the subfloor. Fixing that costs much more than paying for the install.

    Plan on two to four hours if the flange or subfloor needs work, and closer to two for a clean swap with a solid flange. You will have a working toilet the same day. We test before we leave.

    A handle that needs jiggling, a tank that refills on its own, a hiss after the flush or a flush that stops halfway all point to worn tank parts. Each of those parts can be swapped individually. On a toilet past 25 years, replacing the whole fixture often makes more sense.

    Handy homeowners can do it, but the trouble starts when the old toilet comes off and the flange turns out to be cracked or sitting below the new floor. Most DIY leaks start there. Overtightened closet bolts are the other common mistake, and that one cracks a new bowl.

    TOTO, American Standard, Kohler and Gerber all make good toilets, and we steer people toward the models in each line with a 3 inch flush valve and a strong MaP score. The flush design matters more than the name on the tank. We also weigh how easy the parts are to find.

    About two hours for a standard swap. In that time we remove the old toilet, inspect the flange, set the new one and test it, with the old fixture loaded on the truck at the end. Flange repairs add time.
