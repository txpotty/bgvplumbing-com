    What Goes Wrong on a Disposal, and How We Fix It in Knoxville, TN

    Jam Clearing and Motor Repair in Knoxville

    Bones, fruit pits and bottle caps lodge between the grind ring and the impeller lugs, and the motor stalls. We shut off power, insert a 1/4 inch hex key into the socket on the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until the flywheel turns freely. Next we pull the object with pliers. A motor that stalled for a long time may have overheated, so we test it under load before calling the job done.

    Electrical Issues and Motor Repair in Knoxville

    A disposal that hums but will not grind is getting power but is stuck, while one that is silent has no power at all. The two need different fixes. For a silent unit we test the breaker, reset and switch, and for a humming one we check the flywheel and motor before deciding if it is worth repairing.

    Electrical Problems and Resetting in Knoxville

    No hum at all usually means no power reaching the motor. We start at the red reset button on the bottom, then check the breaker, the wall switch and the outlet or junction box under the sink with a meter. It is wet under a sink. In that damp cabinet, wire nuts corrode and cord connections loosen, so we replace them instead of retightening.

    Leaking Disposal Fix in Knoxville, TN

    When the drip starts at the top, the putty under the sink flange is usually dried out. We drop the unit, pull the flange, clean off the old putty and reset it with fresh putty and new gaskets, then hang the disposal back up. That fixes most top leaks. A leak on the side is usually the discharge gasket or a loose hose clamp.

    After-Hours Disposal Service in Knoxville

    A disposal that quits while you are cooking stops everything, especially if the sink is full of water. Skip the drain chemicals. Chemical openers sitting in a disposal can damage the seals and splash back when the plumber opens the trap, so it is better to leave the water and call us.

    Jammed Disposal Repair in Knoxville

    Jams usually trace back to one hard object, like a peach pit, a chicken bone or a twist tie wrapped around the impeller. We free the flywheel from below with the hex key and pull the object with pliers, never a hand, then run it with water to make sure nothing else is stuck. We press the reset after clearing it. Repeat jams point us to the trap arm.

    Preventive Care and Longevity in Knoxville, TN

    Keep cold water running before, during and after use, and let it go about 15 seconds after the motor stops so the food clears the trap. Grease poured in with hot water coats the chamber and the pipe, while cold water keeps it solid so the impellers can break it up. Fibrous scraps jam it most. Throw out celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.

    Humming Disposal Repair in Knoxville

    Humming with no spin almost always means a jam. Bones, pits, glass and spoons are the usual suspects. We cut power, work the flywheel free with a hex wrench on the bottom of the unit, remove the object and then test the motor and the reset button with water running.
