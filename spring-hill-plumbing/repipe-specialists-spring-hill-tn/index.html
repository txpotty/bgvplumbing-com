    Galvanized Pipe Removal in Spring Hill

    Old galvanized lines shed rust flakes that clog aerators, faucet cartridges and toilet fill valves. That is often the first sign. Replacing galvanized with PEX or copper ends the rust, brings pressure back and stops the leaks that start at the threaded joints where galvanized is thinnest. Old pipe stays in walls when pulling it would wreck finished surfaces.

    Drain Line Repiping in Spring Hill

    Drain pipe wears out too, especially old cast iron that cracks and scales up inside. Repeat clogs, sewer smells and several slow drains at once often mean the drain pipe itself is failing. We camera the lines first. Failing sections are replaced with PVC or ABS sloped and vented to code, which drains faster and resists buildup much better than the pipe it replaces.

    Slab Leak Repiping in Spring Hill

    A slab leak shows up as a warm spot on the floor, a running meter or a wet patch of carpet with no visible cause. Crawlspaces are the most common foundation here, though basements are more common in hilly areas where homes are built into slopes. Rather than jackhammer to reach one leak, we run new lines through the attic and walls and leave the old under-slab pipe out of service. No jackhammer needed. It ends this leak and takes the rest of the slab plumbing out of the picture.

    PEX Versus Copper Repipe Pricing in Spring Hill, TN

    PEX repipes usually cost less than copper because the material is cheaper and installation goes faster. Copper costs more. For a typical house, copper often runs 20 to 40 percent above PEX, though some buyers and homeowners like the look and feel of metal pipe and are willing to pay for it. Our quotes for either include permit, fittings, patching and cleanup.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Repipe Specialists in Spring Hill

    Most PEX repipes for Tennessee homes cost $5,000 to $12,000, based on size, bathroom count and access. Copper repipes usually run 20 to 40 percent more.

    A whole-home repipe requires permits, pressure testing and code knowledge. Most homeowners are better served by a licensed crew that can finish fast and pass inspection.

    Both work well, and the best choice depends on your house and your water. PEX is cheaper. PEX is flexible, handles a freeze better and resists scale, while copper is rigid, tolerates sunlight and has a long history, but it costs more and can pinhole in aggressive water.

    For copper, we use domestic Type L from mills such as Mueller and Cerro. For PEX, we use Uponor PEX-A most often. We also install Viega and Zurn PEX on some jobs, but we stick with one brand's pipe and fittings throughout a house so the manufacturer warranty stays valid.

    You get a written workmanship warranty covering labor and materials. Details are spelled out up front. We use Uponor PEX-A and Mueller or Cerro Type L copper, and certified PEX-A installs carry the maker's long-term warranty on top of ours.

    Whole-home repipes require a permit and inspection in most Tennessee jurisdictions. We pull the permit and book the inspections.

    PEX resists scale and pinhole corrosion and handles expansion well, which makes it a strong choice for hard water. Copper lasts but is pricier and can pinhole in corrosive water.

    We commonly use Uponor PEX-A with ProPEX fittings and Type L copper from domestic mills. We also use Viega ProPress fittings for copper work.
