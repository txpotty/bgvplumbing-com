    Frequently Asked Questions About Slab Leak Detection in Franklin

    The cost of slab leak detection in Franklin, TN typically ranges from $250 to $700, depending on the complexity of the leak and the time required for detection. This cost covers the specialized equipment and expertise needed to accurately locate the leak, which is crucial before any repair work can begin.

    Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water with fixtures off, a moving water meter and an unexplained jump in your water bill are common signs. Cracked tile, damp carpet and mildew odors also suggest a slab leak. Call us if you notice any of these.

    A water line slab leak involves pressurized fresh water escaping from supply pipes, often resulting in higher water bills, warm spots or the sound of running water. A sewer line slab leak involves wastewater leaking from drain pipes, which usually causes foul odors, slow drains or visible sewage seepage, and is not usually pressurized.

    Yes, BGV Plumbing stands behind our work. We offer a warranty on our slab leak detection services, guaranteeing the accuracy of our findings. Any subsequent repair work performed by us also comes with a comprehensive warranty on parts and labor, providing you with peace of mind.

    Some homeowner's insurance policies may cover the cost of accessing and repairing the slab leak, particularly if the leak was sudden and accidental. However, coverage often varies, and damage caused by long-term neglect might not be covered. We recommend checking your specific policy details or contacting your insurance provider for details.

    Common pipe materials prone to slab leaks include older copper pipes vulnerable to corrosion from hard water or soil acidity, and sometimes PEX or CPVC if they were improperly installed or exposed to extreme temperatures. Galvanized steel pipes, though less common under slabs today, can also corrode and leak.

    While you can look for the signs, accurately detecting a slab leak requires specialized equipment and expertise that most homeowners don't have. DIY attempts often lead to more damage or misdiagnosis, making professional detection a more cost-effective and reliable solution in the long run.
