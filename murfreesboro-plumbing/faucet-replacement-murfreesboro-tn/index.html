    Kitchen Faucet Replacement in Murfreesboro

    Kitchen faucets usually fail at the hose first. The pull-down hose wears where it bends over the spout neck, and once it splits, water runs down the inside of the faucet and into the cabinet without anyone seeing it. A hose kit can fix that. When the body or cartridge is worn too, we swap the whole faucet and put new supplies on.

    Outdoor Spigot Replacement in Murfreesboro, TN

    Outdoor spigots wear out from sun, hoses and hard closing. Before a freeze, homeowners here usually unhook garden hoses, close crawlspace vents and let faucets drip on outside walls. Worn spigots get replaced, with frost-free models where needed and a vacuum breaker on each, which code calls for on every hose thread. We also check inside the wall. A bib that leaked for months can leave wet insulation behind.

    Tub and Shower Trim Replacement in Murfreesboro, TN

    Tub and shower trim can be replaced without opening the wall. If your valve is a Moen Posi-Temp or a Delta Monitor, a trim kit from the same brand bolts right onto it and gives you new handles, a new spout and a new showerhead. The valve has to match. An outdated valve may need replacing instead.

    Bathroom Faucet Replacement in Murfreesboro, TN

    Old lav faucets rarely come off clean. The mounting nuts sit behind the bowl in a space about the width of a hand, and after twenty years they are usually crusted to the shank. A basin wrench or a cutter gets them off. We replace the pop-up drain at the same time, since the old drain gasket is almost always cracked by then.

    Commercial Kitchen Faucet Replacement in Murfreesboro

    Commercial kitchens are hard on faucets. We replace pre-rinse units and hand sink faucets with commercial-grade models from T&S and Chicago Faucets, which keep parts on the shelf for years. We work around your hours. Every install meets health and accessibility code.

    Corroded Faucet Replacement in Murfreesboro

    Green crust at the base means the faucet body is corroding. Once the brass under the finish is pitted, cartridges stop sealing against it, and every repair drips again within a few weeks. Replacement is the only fix. Even with fused nuts, we get it out and install a faucet with a ceramic disc cartridge.

    Water-Saving Faucet Upgrades in Murfreesboro

    Federal rules cap new kitchen and lav faucets at 2.2 gallons per minute. WaterSense bathroom faucets drop to 1.5 gallons per minute, and the aerated stream feels about the same in your hands. The savings show on the bill. A WaterSense lav faucet is an easy swap.

    Pull-Down Kitchen Faucet Replacement in Murfreesboro

    Pull-down spray heads take a beating. The hose wears, the spray button sticks, and after a few years the docking magnet or weight stops pulling the head back into place. We replace the faucet as a unit. We clip the new hose weight at the right height and connect fresh braided supplies to both stops.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Faucet Replacement in Murfreesboro

    Labor for a faucet replacement usually runs $150 to $350. With a solid mid-range faucet included, most jobs total somewhere between $300 and $750, depending on the model and the condition of the stops. High-end and touchless models can top $800.

    Mineral buildup from harder water can wear out water heaters and leave scale on faucets, showerheads and dishwasher parts. Limescale builds up inside the cartridge and the aerator, which cuts flow and wears the seals until the faucet starts to drip. Handles get stiff too. Faucets with ceramic disc cartridges hold up better, especially if the aerator gets cleaned now and then.

    The old faucet leaves with us. Removal and disposal are part of the price, and brass faucets go to scrap recycling instead of going to the landfill. No cleanup on your end.

    Look under the sink first. Stains on the cabinet floor and crust on the connections usually mean the body leaks, and that is a replacement. Then look at the spout. Cracks in the body or a handle that never fully stops the flow mean it is time.

    We work on every major brand. Most of what we pull and install is Delta, Moen, Kohler, American Standard or Pfister, and we know their hardware well. Off-brand faucets are fine too.

    We can, and we start with a few questions. We ask about the hole count, the clearance above the sink and whether you want a pull-down or a side spray. Budget comes next. We narrow it to a few models that fit.

    Replace it when the body is corroded or cracked. A faucet that leaks after repairs, uses discontinued parts or no longer fits your space should also be replaced. A sound faucet may just need a part.
