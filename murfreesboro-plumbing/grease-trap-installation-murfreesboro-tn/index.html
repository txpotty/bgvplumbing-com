    Grease Trap Sizing and Placement in Murfreesboro, TN

    Sizing starts with fixture drain rates and ends with where the trap can physically go. Long runs to the trap let grease cool and coat the line, so we keep the trap as close to the fixtures as we can. Closer is better. We also check that a pump truck can reach an outdoor tank with about 100 feet of hose, since a tank the hauler cannot reach costs you more on every service.

    Commercial Grease Trap Installation in Murfreesboro

    A commercial kitchen with a three-compartment sink, a pre-rinse station and two floor sinks can push 50 gallons a minute of greasy water at the dinner rush. Our sizing starts from your peak flow, the worst ten minutes of the night, rather than a daily average. Undersized traps are the usual failure. The install covers setting the unit, piping the inlet and outlet, and adding the vented flow control the maker specifies.

    Zurn Grease Trap Installation in Murfreesboro

    Hydromechanical Grease Trap Installation in Murfreesboro

    Flow rating is the number that matters on a hydromechanical trap, and it has to match the fastest-draining fixture combination in your kitchen. A 50 GPM trap on a line that sees 75 GPM will pass grease every lunch rush. We do the math on paper. Each sink compartment's volume, the drain size and a one or two minute drain time give us the GPM figure the code uses.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Murfreesboro

    Installed prices for small indoor hydromechanical traps typically fall between $1,500 and $5,000. Large outdoor gravity interceptors often run $8,000 to $30,000 or more once excavation and site restoration are counted. Brand is a small part of it. The written estimate holds unless the dig turns up a buried surprise.

    Pump-outs every couple of weeks on a trap that used to go two months is the clearest sign you have outgrown it or the baffles have failed. So is a sour grease smell near the floor sinks. Rust shows up too. On steel traps we look for pinholes at the seams, and on any trap we check whether the outlet line downstream is coated with grease that should have been caught.

    An under-sink hydromechanical trap with good access is usually a half-day job, including the flow control and a leak test. Recessing one into the slab adds a day or two for saw-cutting and patching. Outdoor tanks are different. Plan on two to five days for a gravity interceptor once the permit is in hand, depending on depth, soil and how much paving we have to restore.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. For a small indoor trap in a busy kitchen, that can be weekly. Outdoor tanks go longer. Many gravity interceptors are pumped every 30 to 90 days, and your local FOG program may set a maximum interval no matter what the trap looks like.

    Size comes from flow, and flow comes from your fixtures. We add up the drain rate of each sink compartment, floor sink and dish machine, then apply the code's drain-down time to get gallons per minute. Gravity tanks use volume instead. Outdoor tanks get sized by the sewer authority's formula, which we run for you.

    Indoor hydromechanical traps and outdoor gravity interceptors cover nearly every job we do. Automatic grease removal units are a pricier option for kitchens that fry all day. Most cafes go hydromechanical. High-volume kitchens usually get an outdoor gravity tank, especially if the sewer authority asks for one.

    On a commercial kitchen, no. Nearly every jurisdiction requires a licensed plumber to pull the permit and do the work. A homeowner adding a small under-sink trap to a home kitchen may be allowed to do it, but even then the flow control and vent have to be right or the trap siphons and does nothing. Ask the building department first.

    As close to the grease-producing fixtures as the space allows, so grease does not cool and coat the pipe before it reaches the trap.
