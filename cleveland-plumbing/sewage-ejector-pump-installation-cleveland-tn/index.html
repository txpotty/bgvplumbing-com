    What Goes Into an Ejector System in Cleveland, TN

    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Cleveland

    Sump pumps and ejector pumps look alike in a catalog but do very different jobs. A sump pump moves groundwater from a perimeter drain to the yard, while an ejector moves sewage from toilets and sinks to the sewer, and the ejector basin has to be sealed and vented because of the gas. They are not interchangeable.

    Laundry and Wet Bar Ejector Pumps in Cleveland, TN

    Below-grade laundry rooms and wet bars send gray water down that still needs to be lifted to the sewer. A compact gray water pump handles it, sized for the 15 or more gallons a washer drops at once, with a lint filter to keep fibers off the impeller. Lint kills small pumps.

    Liberty Pumps Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Cleveland, TN

    Liberty builds single and dual pump packages that arrive with the basin, pump, lid and fittings matched. That speeds up new installs, and their wide-angle float switches are less likely to hang up on the basin wall than older tethered floats, which is one of the most common reasons a pump fails to start. Stuck floats are common.

    Ejector Pump Installation Cost Comparison in Cleveland, TN

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin costs the least, often done in a few hours. A new system with a basin, slab excavation, venting through the roof, electrical work and an alarm costs more, but it is the only way to add a bathroom below the sewer line in most homes without a macerating unit. Ask for both numbers.

    Upflush and Macerating Toilet Systems in Cleveland

    Macerating systems like Saniflo add a bath fixture or two without breaking the concrete floor. The unit sits behind the toilet, grinds the waste and pumps it through a small discharge line, often three-quarter inch or one inch, up to the nearest drain, which makes it a good fit for a small basement half bath. Venting still applies.

    Cost-Effective Sewage Ejector Pump Upgrades in Cleveland, TN

    Upgrading an old ejector does not always mean a new basin. With a sound basin, a new pump, fresh check valve, shutoff and alarm give you most of a new system for far less. We tell you if the basin is done.

    Residential Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Cleveland

    Residential ejector systems in Cleveland serve basement bathrooms, lower-level suites and any fixture that sits too low to drain by gravity to the sewer. Each system needs a sealed basin, a pump with a float, a check valve and a vent. Simple parts, exact install. Most problems come from the install, not the hardware, so we take our time on the vent and the lid seal.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Basin & Pit Installation in Cleveland

    Digging the pit starts with a saw cut through the floor, deep enough to set the basin flush with the finished floor. Karst ground with sinkholes is common in parts of Tennessee, and shifting soil above that rock can break buried pipes over time. The basin sits on gravel with backfill around it so rising groundwater cannot float it, and new concrete seals the floor and locks it down. Floating basins crack floors.
