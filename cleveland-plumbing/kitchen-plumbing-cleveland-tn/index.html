    Disposal Repair and Replacement in Cleveland, TN

    A disposal that hums but will not spin usually has something wedged in it. We cut the power, work the flywheel loose with a 1/4 inch hex key from underneath, pull out the bottle cap or bone, and press the red reset button before we run it again. Leaking units are another matter. A drip from the bottom of the housing means the internal seal failed, and that unit has to be replaced.

    Disposal Replacement in Cleveland, TN

    Disposals last about eight to twelve years. Water dripping from the bottom of the housing means it is finished. We drop the old unit, check the mounting ring, install the new one and reconnect the dishwasher hose using a fresh clamp so it does not leak at the inlet. The old unit leaves with us.

    Grease Trap Repair in Cleveland, TN

    Rusted inlet tees, cracked lids and missing baffles all let grease pass straight through a trap. We inspect each part during cleaning and replace what has failed, usually on the same visit. A trap missing its baffle is just a pipe. On big interceptors we schedule the pump-out first and then check the walls, outlet and sampling port.

    Disposal Jams and Hums in Cleveland, TN

    Humming with no grinding means the motor is getting power but the plate is stuck. That is often ten minutes. We free the plate from below with a hex key, clear the hopper with pliers, never a hand, and reset the overload before we test it with water. Silence with no hum points to the switch, the outlet or the breaker instead.

    Private wells serve many rural homes in Tennessee, and some well water carries iron, sulfur or sediment that calls for filtration or treatment. The aerator is where you see scale first. We can also test your water hardness and price a softener or an under-sink filter.
