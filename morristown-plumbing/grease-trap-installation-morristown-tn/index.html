    What Goes Into a Grease Trap Installation in Morristown, TN

    Residential Grease Interceptor Setup in Morristown, TN

    Homes rarely need a grease trap, though some do. Licensed home bakeries and houses on old clay laterals that keep clogging are the usual cases. A small 4 to 7 GPM unit under the kitchen sink catches most of it, and cleaning takes about ten minutes with a spatula and a bucket. Check with your local health office first.

    New Restaurant Build-Out Grease Traps in Morristown

    On a new build-out, the grease trap gets decided before the slab is poured, not after. The drawings and the equipment list from your kitchen designer tell us what will drain where. Changes get expensive. Saw-cutting a finished floor to relocate a trap can add several days to a build-out, so we flag sizing or location problems at plan review.

    Grease Trap Installation Cost in Morristown, TN

    Price follows size and location more than brand. The low end is a small indoor unit with easy access and a short pipe run. The high end is an outdoor gravity interceptor with excavation, a traffic-rated lid and a permit, which can run five figures once concrete restoration is counted. We quote after a site visit.

    Food Truck Commissary and Ghost Kitchen Traps in Morristown, TN

    Commissary kitchens see a dozen trucks dumping wash water in the same two hours, which is a different load from a restaurant spreading it over a long shift. Sizing has to cover that short, heavy spike. Shared delivery-only kitchens can have more sinks per square foot than any dining room, and the trap must handle all of them. Each brand's prep sink counts.

    Under-Sink Grease Trap Setup in Morristown, TN

    Small cafes and coffee bars often get by with a 10 to 25 GPM hydromechanical trap tucked under the prep sink or beside it. The unit goes on the floor or in a shallow recess, and the flow control fitting goes upstream. Clearance matters most. Your staff needs to lift the lid and clean it weekly, so we leave space to do that without unbolting anything.

    Zurn Grease Trap Installation in Morristown

    Zurn's indoor grease interceptors cover the common sizes from small under-sink units up to 100 GPM, and we set enough of them to know where the gaskets give trouble. After a few years of hot water and scraping, the lid gasket is what usually leaks. We keep spares on the truck. We mount the supplied flow control on the inlet, at the height the instructions list, and vent it.

    Replacement and Upgrade Services in Morristown, TN

    Steel traps from decades back rust through at the baffle seams, and by then the outlet is usually passing grease straight to the sewer. We run a camera down the outlet first to see how much grease got past the old unit. Sometimes the line needs jetting too. Upgrading also gives you a chance to move from a 20 GPM trap to a 35 or 50 if the menu or seat count grew.

    Hydro-Mechanical Grease Interceptor Installation in Morristown

    Inside a hydromechanical interceptor, baffles do the separating while a flow control fitting with an air intake holds incoming water to the rated flow. We pipe the air intake the way the manufacturer and the code both require. Get that wrong and the trap siphons. We also set the unit level within a quarter inch, since a tilted trap short-circuits the baffles and loses capacity.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Morristown

    Small under-sink hydromechanical traps usually land between $1,000 and $3,000 installed, and buried interceptors with excavation commonly run $5,000 to $20,000 or more. Most of the difference is labor, mainly digging and concrete repair. Permits add a little. We quote in writing after a site visit, with the trap, the labor and the permit fees listed as separate lines.

    Two warranties apply here. Manufacturer coverage on the unit varies by brand and material, and plastic bodies usually get the longer terms. Our labor warranty covers the joints and the setting, and you get it in writing up front.

    We start with a fixture list and the drain rate of each fixture. Each sink's volume and drain time give a flow rate, and the dish machine's spec sheet gives its flow. That gives the GPM figure. Hydromechanical traps are picked by that GPM rating, while gravity interceptors are sized by volume and retention time from the same data.

    Plan on a permit. Grease interceptors are regulated by both the plumbing code and the local sewer use ordinance, so two offices may want to see the plans. We file both when needed and give you copies of the approvals, which your health inspector may ask to see at opening. Unpermitted traps cause trouble at lease transfer.

    The common rule is to clean before grease and solids fill 25 percent of the trap's depth. Busy kitchens with small indoor traps often hit that mark every week. Outdoor tanks go longer. A gravity interceptor typically gets pumped monthly to quarterly, and some sewer programs set the schedule for you.

    Half a day for most indoor units. That covers setting the trap, piping the inlet and outlet, installing the flow control and running a water test. Outdoor tanks need two to five days, with an inspection before backfill. Permits add lead time.

    For indoor traps we lean on Zurn, Watts, Schier and Endura, because their parts are easy to find and their units carry PDI or ASME certification. Schier's plastic bodies do not rust, which helps in hot, wet kitchens. Brand matters less than sizing. A well-known trap installed too small will still fail inspection, so we pick the size first and the brand second.

    Watch the pump-out bills. Rising service frequency means the trap has been outgrown or the baffles have failed. Smell is another clue. Slow floor sinks, grease odor at the trap, a lid that no longer seals, or a failed FOG inspection all point the same way, and we can tell you which it is in one visit.
