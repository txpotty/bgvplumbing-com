    Sump Pump Installation Cost Comparison in Morristown, TN

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit is the least expensive option, usually a few hundred dollars plus the pump. Digging a new pit through the slab adds labor for saw cutting, excavation and concrete patching. Adding a battery backup increases the total but often costs less than one cleanup after a flooded basement. We give you an itemized quote so you can compare a basic primary pump against a full primary and backup system before we start.

    High-Capacity Sump Pump Solutions in Morristown, TN

    For homes with significant groundwater issues or large basements, a standard sump pump may not be enough. BGV Plumbing installs high-capacity sump pump systems designed to move larger volumes of water quickly and reliably. These systems often feature more powerful motors and larger discharge lines.     Commercial and Multi-Unit Sump Installations in Morristown

    Office buildings, parking structures and multi-unit properties with below-grade levels usually need duplex sump systems with two pumps and an alternating control panel. The panel switches lead and lag pumps on each cycle so wear stays even, and a high water alarm alerts building staff. We coordinate electrical with your electrician and follow manufacturer specs for basin size and float settings. Property managers get a simple maintenance schedule to keep both pumps exercised.

    Sump Pump Basin and Discharge Line Installation in Morristown

    A complete sump pump system requires a properly installed basin and discharge line. We handle the excavation and placement of the sump basin, setting it at the lowest point of your basement floor so it collects water well. We also install the discharge line, ensuring it is correctly sloped, sealed and routed away from your foundation to a suitable drainage area, so water does not re-enter your Morristown property.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Morristown

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by finding the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor remains clean and level.
