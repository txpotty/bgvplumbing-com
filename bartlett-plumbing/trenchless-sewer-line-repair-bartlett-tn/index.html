    Causes of Sewer Line Damage in Bartlett, TN

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints separate, crack or drop out of grade over the years. Karst ground with sinkholes is common in parts of Tennessee, and shifting soil above that rock can crack buried pipes over time. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Then age finishes the job. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under load.

    Pipe Bursting in Bartlett, TN

    Two pits are standard for a burst, one at the house end near the foundation and one out near the street or the city tap. The new HDPE pipe is butt-fused into one length first. The winch or hydraulic puller then drags the bursting head and the new pipe through. It goes quickly.

    Emergency Trenchless Sewer Services in Bartlett

    A sudden collapse usually shows up as every drain in the house backing up at once, often into the lowest tub or floor drain. The first job is getting flow back, usually with a cable or a jetter. Then the camera shows whether the line can be lined or needs a burst. Some collapses need a small dig.

    Sectional Point Repair in Bartlett

    A point repair is a short liner, often 3 to 5 feet long, set over one bad spot. We push it in on a packer, guided by the camera footage, and the packer inflates to press the resin-soaked sleeve against the pipe wall until it cures. One crack, one patch. Sound lines with one bad joint are the right candidates.

    Cast Iron Sewer Line Repair in Bartlett, TN

    Cast iron rusts from the inside out, and the bottom of the pipe goes first. That is called channel rot. We descale the pipe with chain knockers or carbide cutters spinning on a flexible shaft, which brings the wall back to bare metal so a liner can bond to it. Under a slab, that beats jackhammering.
