    Pinholes, Joints and Fittings, Repaired in Bartlett, TN

    Pinhole Leak Repair in Bartlett

    Pinholes start inside the pipe where the copper wall pits and thins. You see a drip or a stain. They are small and easy to miss behind drywall, so we locate them with a thermal camera and moisture meter before cutting a single opening in the wall or ceiling. We replace the pitted length with Type L. Nearby pipe gets checked.

    Copper Pipe Joint Leak Repair in Bartlett, TN

    Joints leak from poor original soldering, thermal expansion or a house that settles. Karst ground with sinkholes is common in parts of Tennessee, and shifting soil above that rock can crack buried pipes over time. A crusty fitting is the clue. We inspect each joint near the leak for weeping or mineral buildup, then clean, reflux and resolder the joint, or replace the fitting entirely with a new sweat or press fitting. We test before closing up.

    Copper Fitting and Valve Repair in Bartlett

    Angle stops, shutoff valves and copper fittings under sinks and behind toilets can weep or seize. Minerals jam them. Hard water buildup inside a valve can keep it from closing when you need it most, and quarter-turn valves go in to replace the old multi-turn stops. Every valve gets checked.

    Preventive Copper Pipe Inspection in Bartlett, TN

    An inspection catches thin spots before they leak. Plan on an hour. We look at exposed copper for green crust and pitting, test static pressure, check the bond wire at the water heater and main, and run a thermal scan on walls where pipe runs if the house has had leaks before. We write up what we find.

    Solder Joint Leak Repair in Bartlett, TN

    Older solder joints can fail from poor original work, thermal expansion or vibration. Crusty fittings are a clue. A leaking joint usually weeps for a while before it drips, so we either resolder it or replace the fitting and a short length of pipe. The joint gets tested.

    Hot Water Copper Line Repair in Bartlett, TN

    Hot water lines corrode faster than cold lines. Temperature drives it. Recirculation pumps that run around the clock push water through the copper at high velocity and wear it from the inside, so we repair leaking hot lines and can add a timer or adjust the pump to cut down on wear. Less run time, less wear.
