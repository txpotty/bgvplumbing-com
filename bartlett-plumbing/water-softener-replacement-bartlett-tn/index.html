    Metered Softener Replacement in Bartlett

    Older timer-based softeners regenerate on a set schedule whether you used water or not. Metered softeners track actual usage and regenerate only when the resin is nearly used up.     Frequently Asked Questions About Water Softener Replacement in Bartlett

    A standard single tank softener replacement usually costs $1,500 to $3,000 installed. Dual tank or premium systems usually run $3,000 to $5,000. Adding a new loop or drain line can add to the cost.

    In many areas, a plumbing permit is usually not required for a direct like-for-like water softener replacement if no significant changes to the existing plumbing are made. However, if new water lines or drain lines are being installed or relocated, a permit may be required. We will advise you on any permitting requirements.

    New water softeners usually come with two types of warranties. There is a manufacturer's warranty on the equipment itself, which can range from 5 to 10 years or more for tanks and control valves, and an installation warranty from BGV Plumbing. We stand behind our work with a solid warranty for your peace of mind.

    Common signs include a return of hard water symptoms like scale buildup on fixtures, dry skin and hair, dingy laundry or excessive soap use. Other signs are a noticeable drop in water pressure, leaks from the unit, or a softener over 10 to 15 years old that needs frequent repairs.

    Most softeners last 10 to 15 years. Resin and valves wear out sooner with very hard or chlorinated water.

    Spots on dishes, scale on faucets, dry skin and increased soap use can point to a failing softener. A softener that no longer uses salt or keeps flooding the brine tank may also need replacement.

    Most softener replacements take two to four hours. New loops or drain lines can take longer.

    Salt-based softeners remove hardness minerals, calcium and magnesium, through ion exchange, effectively softening the water. Salt-free conditioners do not remove minerals but alter their crystal structure so they do not stick to surfaces. That prevents scale buildup without actually softening the water.
