    Clay and Orangeburg Sewer Lines in Bristol

    Older properties may still have clay or Orangeburg pipe. Each fails in its own way. Clay joints invite roots, and Orangeburg, a tar-paper pipe from the mid-1900s, flattens under the weight of the soil over time, so we clean these lines carefully and map the joints and soft spots on camera.

    Cast Iron Sewer Cleaning in Bristol, TN

    Cast iron rots from the inside. Housing in Tennessee ranges from older homes with cast iron drains and galvanized supply lines to newer builds plumbed with PEX. The bottom of the pipe wears first. Because a heavy cutter can punch through weak iron, we use chain descalers and a lower-pressure jet, then check the wall thickness on camera.

    Residential Sewer Cleaning in Bristol

    Household sewers clog slowly. Grease, hair and wipes build up over months. Debris catches at a rough joint or a sag, and every load of laundry adds to it until the line closes, and we clear it with a sectional cable or a small jetter sized for the pipe.

    Grease and Scale Buildup Cleaning in Bristol, TN

    Grease bonds with mineral scale into a hard crust. Water hardness in Tennessee varies by source, and areas that draw from limestone aquifers or springs tend to have fairly hard water. A cable can punch a hole through the crust, but a jetter cleans the walls back to full size and leaves less for new grease to grab onto, and the method depends on pipe material and condition.
