    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Cleaning in Memphis

    Small under-sink traps often start around $200 a visit. Large outdoor interceptors can pass $1,000 when they are full and the cap has hardened. Disposal is billed by the gallon at the receiving facility, so the gallons hauled drive most of the cost, and a 1,500-gallon tank fills a truck.

    Zurn, Schier, Watts and Big Dipper units turn up across Tennessee every week, along with Rockford traps and older steel boxes from makers that are long gone. Parts are usually available. On discontinued models, a universal baffle or flow control often fits, and if the steel has rusted through we quote a replacement.

    The terms get mixed up constantly. Most people use trap for the small indoor unit, usually under 100 gallons. An interceptor is the big in-ground tank outside, often 500 gallons or more, and plumbing codes treat them as separate categories with different sizing rules.

    Rules come from your sewer district or city pretreatment program. Most follow some version of the 25 percent rule. Plenty also cap the interval, want a year or more of records on site, and ban enzymes in place of pumping, so we look up your program before we schedule.

    Fryer volume drives the schedule more than seat count. Plenty of busy restaurants pump monthly. Lighter kitchens, like a coffee shop with a small food menu, can stretch to every two or three months as long as depth readings stay under the 25 percent line.

    Yes, every visit. The sheet shows the date, gallons and depth readings. It also notes baffle and tee condition and names the hauler and receiving facility, which covers what most pretreatment inspectors ask to see.

    A small under-sink trap can be hand-cleaned by staff. The waste still has to go somewhere legal. In most places you cannot put trap waste in the dumpster or pour it down another drain, and outdoor interceptors need a vacuum truck and a licensed hauler no matter what.

    No permit is needed just to have a trap pumped. The records are your responsibility. A new or resized trap is different, usually needing a plumbing permit and sometimes a review by the pretreatment program first.
