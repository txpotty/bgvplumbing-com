    Sump Pump Types We Install in Memphis, TN

    Pedestal Sump Pump Replacement in Memphis, TN

    Pedestal pumps keep the motor up on a column above the pit, out of the water, which is why a good one can run fifteen years in a dry basement. They are louder, no question. The new column gets set plumb and braced, and we set the float rod stops so the pump turns on and off at the right water levels. Narrow pits, under about 12 inches across, are where they make the most sense.

    Cast Iron Submersible Pumps in Memphis

    Cast iron sheds motor heat into the water around it far better than a plastic housing does, which is a big part of why a Zoeller M53 or Liberty 250 outlasts a hardware-store pump. Most swaps take one to two hours. We also check that the float has room to travel without touching the basin wall.

    Emergency Sump Pump Replacement in Memphis, TN

    A pit that is filling with the pump silent means you have maybe an hour or two before water crosses the basement floor, depending on how fast it is coming in. Check the breaker and the GFCI outlet first, then lift the lid and see whether the float is stuck. A shop vac or a bucket can hold the level down. We stock the common pumps on the truck, and most emergency swaps are done on the first trip.

    Sump Basin and Pit Replacement in Memphis, TN

    Old clay tile or 18-inch plastic basins crack and fill with silt, and a pump in a basin that small cycles every few minutes during a heavy rain. We break out the concrete, set a larger poly basin with a sealed lid, and pour the floor back. More volume in the pit means fewer starts per hour. Fewer starts, longer motor life.

    Wayne Sump Pump Swaps in Memphis

    Wayne makes everything from a thermoplastic quarter-horse pump at the hardware store to cast iron models with vertical switches, and the cheap ones are what we replace most. The usual failure is a burned-out motor or a float that hangs up on the cord. We replace it with the right Wayne or another cast iron unit. Takes about two hours.

    Pedestal Pump Service in Memphis, TN

    Louder, yes, but a pedestal motor sits high and dry, so in a narrow pit it often outlasts a submersible by several years. Narrow pits favor pedestals. We replace worn units and set the float rod stops for a proper cycle. A submersible is worth a look when the pit is wide enough.

    High-Capacity Sump Pump Upgrades in Memphis

    If your pump runs every 30 seconds during a storm and still loses ground, it is undersized for the water coming in. We time the cycles and measure how fast the pit refills. From there it is math. Often a 1/2 or 3/4 HP pump with more GPM at your lift, plus a 2-inch discharge line, solves it.

    Sizing a Submersible Sump Pump in Memphis

    Horsepower matters less than people think. For most houses a 1/3 HP pump is enough, provided the water only has to climb 10 to 15 feet to get out. Before we choose, we read the pump curve at your real discharge height. A pump that is too big empties the pit too fast and wears itself out.

    Sump Pump Replacement Near Me in Memphis, TN

    Groundwater levels shift with the seasons, and in a wet spring a pit can take on water every few minutes even with no rain in the forecast. Our sizing is based on the wettest week of the year. Not for an average day.

    Speed counts on a sump call. A basement can take on several inches of water in an hour when a pump quits during a heavy storm, so we send the closest available tech. Most arrive with the right pump.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sump Pump Replacement in Memphis

    Expect $500 to $1,500 for the pump and labor. Cutting in a new pit or rerouting the discharge line can add several hundred dollars to the total. The quote comes after we look at the pit.

    Plan on 7 to 10 years from a decent pump. A pump that sits idle most of the year tends to die early, since seals dry out and switches stick. A quick bucket test every quarter catches it.

    Manufacturer coverage on the pump usually runs 1 to 3 years for plastic models and up to 5 for cast iron. We add our own labor warranty. We go over the terms before you pick a pump.

    The primary pump runs on household current and does the daily work. A backup pump, battery or water driven, sits higher in the pit and only runs when the main pump dies, the power drops or the water comes in too fast. It is cheap insurance.

    One to two hours for a straight swap. Adding a battery backup or a new discharge line takes longer.

    Budget a morning or an afternoon. The work includes pulling the old pump, cleaning the basin, installing the new pump and check valve, and running a fill test. Longer if the basin or discharge needs work.

    Two to four hours covers most standard swaps. Cutting in a new basin or running a new discharge line outside can push the job to a full day. We will tell you which kind of job it is before we start.
