    Tank Leaks and Anode Rods in Memphis

    Corrosion starts at the anode rod and works its way into the steel once the rod is spent. Mineral buildup from harder water can wear out water heaters and leave scale on faucets, showerheads and dishwasher parts. We pull the anode, check how much magnesium or aluminum is left and replace it if the core wire is showing. A fresh anode, a drained tank and a relief valve that seats properly can add years to a heater in a Memphis home that has not started leaking from the shell. Water from the shell seam is another story. At that point the tank has to go.

    Gas Water Heater Code Checks in Memphis, TN

    Fuel gas code covers more of a water heater repair than most people expect. The tech looks for a drip leg, a listed flex connector and an accessible shutoff valve. Garage installs in Memphis, TN need the burner raised 18 inches off the floor unless the heater is listed as vapor resistant, and strapping goes on wherever the local code asks for it. Clearances get measured too.

    Pilot Light Repair in Memphis

    The thermocouple is the first thing we test when a pilot will not stay lit. With a millivolt meter, it takes about a minute to see if it is putting out enough. If it reads fine, the next suspects are a clogged pilot orifice, a cracked piezo wire or a thermal cutoff that tripped because the combustion chamber overheated from blocked air. Most of these jobs run about an hour.

    Gas Control Valve Replacement in Memphis, TN

    Honeywell and White-Rodgers valves report faults with a blinking LED, and the flash count matches a chart on the label. We read the code, then test the thermopile and sensors before blaming the valve. Valves do fail. When the control itself is the problem, we replace it with an exact-match valve or a listed universal model, set the temperature and leak test every joint on the gas train before relighting. You get the old valve back if you want it.

    Propane Water Heater Repair in Memphis

    Propane and natural gas heaters look the same but burn at different pressures. A propane tank needs its own orifice and a valve set for roughly 11 inches of water column. Natural gas runs closer to 7 inches, so a heater converted badly, or a regulator that drifted, gives you soot, a yellow flame and a pilot that keeps going out for no obvious reason. A manometer reading tells us the supply pressure. A bad conversion gets corrected with the right parts.

    Burner Assembly and Venting Repair in Memphis

    Rust scale on the burner tray is a sign the flue baffle or the tank top is shedding. We pull the burner, clear the ports and look at the flame arrestor screen underneath. Then the vent gets checked from the draft hood to the roof, because a flue that is loose, sagging or capped by a bird nest can push carbon monoxide back into your Memphis home. A smoke pencil shows the draft.

    All Major Gas Water Heater Brands Repaired in Memphis

    Every brand label we see in garages and closets uses one of a handful of gas control designs. Rheem and Ruud share most parts, and State shares a lot with A.O. Smith.     Pilot Light and Ignition Repair in Memphis

    No hot water and a dark pilot window usually point to the ignition side of the heater. We pull the pilot assembly and look at the thermocouple tip, the pilot orifice and the piezo igniter wire. A thermocouple that tests weak on the millivolt meter will not hold the gas valve's magnet open, so the pilot drops out a minute after you relight it by the label. Common pilot kits ride on most of our trucks.
