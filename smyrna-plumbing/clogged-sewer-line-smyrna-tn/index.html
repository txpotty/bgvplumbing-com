    Sewer Belly and Sagging Pipe Clogs in Smyrna, TN

    Crawlspaces are the most common foundation here, though basements are more frequent in hilly areas where homes are built into slopes. A section of sewer pipe that sinks forms a belly. Waste settles in the low spot. Over time that standing water collects paper and grease, and the line clogs at the same spot every few months no matter how often it gets cleaned. Our camera and locator show where the belly is and how deep it sits. Then we quote a spot repair.

    Cleaning vs. Repairing a Sewer Line in Smyrna, TN

    A sound pipe with a grease or root clog just needs cleaning. Broken pipe needs repair. Offset joints, cracks and collapsed sections keep clogging no matter how often they get cleared, so the camera footage decides whether you spend a few hundred dollars on cleaning or more on a spot fix, liner or replacement. We walk you through it.

    Hydro-Jetting for Stubborn Clogs in Smyrna

    Some lines get cabled three times a year and still clog. Cabling alone is not enough. Those lines are usually coated in grease or scale, and the cable only bores a narrow path through the middle that closes up again within weeks. Jetting removes the coating down to the pipe wall. The results hold up far better.

    Sewer Camera Inspection in Smyrna

    A sewer camera is a waterproof head on a push rod with a locator transmitter built in. We see what the pipe looks like. Cracks, offsets, sags, roots and heavy scale all show up on the screen, while the locator marks the spot and depth from above. That keeps digging to a minimum. You get a copy of the footage.

    Sewer Cleanout Installation in Smyrna

    Some older homes have no cleanout at all. That makes every clog harder. Without one, a plumber has to pull a toilet or climb on the roof to reach the main, and a two-way cleanout by the foundation fixes that. Future clearing gets cheaper and faster.

    Main Line vs. Branch Line Clogs in Smyrna, TN

    One slow drain is a branch clog. More than one usually means the main. Water rising in the shower when you flush is a strong sign the clog is past the point where those two lines join, which is why we ask which fixtures are acting up before we decide where to open the line. Starting in the right place matters.
