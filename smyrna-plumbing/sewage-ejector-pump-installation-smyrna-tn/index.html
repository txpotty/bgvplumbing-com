    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Smyrna

    Replacing a pump in an existing basin usually costs $900 to $2,000. A new ejector system with basin, venting and floor work typically ranges from $2,500 to $6,000 or more.

    Common signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include the pump running constantly, not turning on at all, making unusual grinding or loud noises, or foul odors or sewage backing up into your basement fixtures. These symptoms point to a failing unit that needs immediate attention.

    A standard sewage ejector pump installation usually takes one to two days. This includes excavating the pit, setting the basin and pump, connecting the plumbing and completing the electrical hookups. More complex installations, like those requiring significant trenching or electrical panel upgrades, may take longer.

    A sump pump is designed to remove groundwater from a basement or crawl space, preventing flooding. A sewage ejector pump, however, is specifically engineered to handle wastewater containing solids from toilets, sinks and showers in areas below the main sewer line. They are not interchangeable and serve different purposes.

    We primarily install high-quality, reputable brands such as Liberty Pumps and Zoeller. These manufacturers are known for their durability, reliability and efficient performance, ensuring a long-lasting solution for your home. We can discuss the best brand and model for your needs.

    Installing a sewage ejector pump is a complex job that involves plumbing, electrical and sometimes excavation work. It requires specialized knowledge to get sizing, venting and code compliance right. We strongly recommend hiring a licensed professional like BGV Plumbing to avoid costly errors, safety hazards and potential code violations.

    With proper installation and regular maintenance, a high-quality sewage ejector pump can last 7 to 15 years. Factors such as usage frequency, the type of waste it handles and water quality can influence its lifespan. Annual inspections can help maximize its longevity.

    Avoid flushing wipes, paper towels, feminine products, grease and cat litter. These items clog pumps and cause premature failure.
