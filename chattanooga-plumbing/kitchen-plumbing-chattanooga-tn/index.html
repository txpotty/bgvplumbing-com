    Sinks, Disposals and Drains in Chattanooga, TN: How We Handle Them

    Replacing an Old Kitchen Sink in Chattanooga

    Pulling an old cast iron sink takes two people and a putty knife, since they are heavy and usually caulked in tight. The counter cutout gets measured first. A new sink has to match that opening or the counter has to be trimmed, and we tell you which before you buy. The drain gets rebuilt to suit the new bowl depth.

    Grease Trap Installation in Chattanooga, TN

    Under-sink grease traps work for small kitchens, while full restaurants often need an in-ground interceptor sized to the local sewer authority's rules. The local rule gets checked before we price it. Sizing is based on fixture flow, and an undersized trap fills fast and lets grease pass straight into the sewer line.

    InSinkErator Installation in Chattanooga, TN

    Many homeowners ask to step up from an older InSinkErator to an Evolution because the newer units are quieter and grind finer. The mount often carries over. When the old Quick Lock mount is sound, we can reuse it, which saves time and leaves the sink flange alone, and we register the warranty for you.

    Disposal Repair and Replacement in Chattanooga, TN

    A disposal that hums but will not spin is almost always jammed. We cut the power, work the flywheel loose with a 1/4 inch hex key from underneath, pull out the bottle cap or bone, and press the red reset button before we run it again. Leaks are a different story. A drip from the bottom of the housing means the internal seal failed, and that unit has to be replaced.

    Dishwasher and Ice Maker Lines in Chattanooga

    A dishwasher needs a hot supply with its own shutoff, a drain to the disposal or a wye tailpiece, and either an air gap at the sink or a high loop under the counter, depending on local code. Fridges need a cold line. We use copper or braided stainless with a quarter-turn valve, not the thin plastic tubing that cracks behind refrigerators.

    Disposal Jams and Hums in Chattanooga, TN

    A hum without grinding tells us the motor has power and the plate will not turn. That is often ten minutes. We free the plate from below with a hex key, clear the hopper with pliers, never a hand, and reset the overload before we test it with water. Silence with no hum points to the switch, the outlet or the breaker instead.

A trap missing its baffle is just a pipe. On big interceptors we schedule the pump-out first and then check the walls, outlet and sampling port.
