    Causes of Sewer Line Damage in Nashville, TN

    Shifting soil is one of the main reasons sewer joints pull apart or crack. Crawlspaces are the most common foundation here, though basements are more frequent in hilly areas where homes are built into slopes. Roots are the other big one, since a hairline gap at a clay joint is enough for a feeder root to get in and then swell into a mat that catches paper. Age does the rest. Cast iron scales and thins, and clay bells crack under load.

    Emergency Trenchless Sewer Repair in Nashville

    Cured-In-Place Pipe Lining in Nashville

    Prep is most of the job. We jet the line to remove roots and grease, then camera it again to measure the exact footage before cutting the liner tube to length. A poor prep job means a wrinkled liner. Lining works best on cracked or root-intruded lines that still hold their round shape.

    Orangeburg and Clay Pipe Repair in Nashville

    Orangeburg is bituminous fiber pipe that was laid in houses mostly from the late 1940s into the early 1970s. It goes oval and blisters, so a liner has nothing round to bond to and bursting is usually the better fix. Clay is different. Vitrified clay that still holds shape lines well.

    Root Intrusion Fixes in Nashville, TN

    Roots enter at the joints, where clay bells or cast iron hubs have a small gap or a failed gasket. Once inside, they branch into a mat that fills the pipe. We cut them out with a mechanical root cutter or a hydro jet, then line the pipe so those joints are sealed for good and the line stops needing a cleaning every few months. With no joints left, there is nowhere for them to return.

We work through cleanouts and roof vents, so the drywall and tile stay where they are. Branch lines get reinstated with a robotic cutter. That saves opening a finished wall.
