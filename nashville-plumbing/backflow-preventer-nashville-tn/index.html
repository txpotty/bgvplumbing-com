    Febco Backflow Preventer Experts in Nashville

    Febco assemblies are common on irrigation and commercial lines. The 765 PVB, the 850 double check and the 825Y and 860 RPZs are the models we see most. Every model has quirks. Air inlet poppets fail on the 765, and relief diaphragms are the weak spot on the 825Y.

    Commercial Backflow Services in Nashville, TN

    Fire Line Backflow Testing in Nashville

    Frequently Asked Questions About Backflow Preventer in Nashville

    A standard backflow test in Nashville, TN usually runs $75 to $150. Rebuilds tend to fall between $200 and $600 depending on which parts fail, while a new assembly can run $450 to $1,500 or more on a larger commercial line. Pipe size matters most. You get a quote before any work starts.

    Water providers may send follow-up notices, charge fees or eventually suspend service. Booking early heads all of that off. Rush tests are often possible within a day or two of your call.

    Testing must be done by a certified backflow tester using calibrated equipment. Providers reject anything else. A recent calibration date is part of every report.

    Watts, Febco, Zurn Wilkins, Apollo and other common brands. Our trucks carry rebuild kits for the popular models. Less common parts can usually be ordered within a few business days, and we schedule the rebuild as soon as they arrive.

    We service reduced pressure zone assemblies, double check valve assemblies, pressure vacuum breakers and atmospheric vacuum breakers. Watts, Wilkins, Febco and Conbraco are the most common, and we also see Apollo and Zurn on commercial work. Common kits ride along.

    A standard test usually takes 20 to 45 minutes per assembly. Water is briefly shut off during the test, so we let you know before we close the valve on the supply side. A rebuild adds about an hour.

    Look for a steady leak from the device, a sudden drop in water pressure or discolored water at the tap. On an RPZ, water spitting from the relief port every time pressure changes is a sign a check is leaking back. Get it tested soon.

    Ten to twenty years is common for a well-kept brass assembly. How long it lasts depends on the water, the weather it sits in and the kind of device. Before a freeze, homeowners here usually disconnect hoses, close crawlspace vents and let faucets drip on outside walls. Testing catches wear early.
