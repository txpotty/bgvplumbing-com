    What We Install in Nashville, TN

    Backflow Preventer Installation in Nashville

    Irrigation systems and fire sprinkler lines are the usual reasons a home needs a backflow preventer. It keeps fertilizer-laced lawn water from being pulled back into the drinking supply when a main break or hydrant flush drops pressure. The type matters. We pick the assembly for the hazard level and local requirement and test it on install day.

    Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Nashville, TN

    A basement bathroom sits below the sewer line, so gravity cannot carry the waste out. An ejector pump in a sealed basin lifts it up to the main drain. We size the pump and basin to the fixtures it serves, vent the basin to code, and put a check valve and a full-port shutoff on the discharge so the pump can be pulled for service. Brands we trust here are Liberty and Zoeller.

    Water Heater Installation in Nashville

    Tank or tankless, gas or electric, the first question is how many people shower in the morning. Sizing starts with your peak morning demand and the fuel you have, and we look at venting and gas supply before giving a number. Gas units get the most attention. A draft hood that spills or a flue with the wrong pitch can push carbon monoxide back into the house. We haul the old tank away before we leave.

    Water Line Installation and Replacement in Nashville

    Corroded galvanized and brittle polybutylene are the two pipes we replace most. New lines go in as PEX or Type L copper, with crimp or expansion fittings on the plastic and solder on the copper. Karst ground with sinkholes is common in parts of Tennessee, and shifting soil above that rock can crack buried pipes over time. Buried pipe gets sand bedding and proper depth, so rocks and settling do not cut into it later. Street pressure over 80 psi gets a PRV.

    Tankless Water Heater Installation in Nashville, TN

    Navien and Rinnai tankless units hang on a wall and free up the floor space a tank used to take. Gas models usually need a bigger gas line and new stainless or PVC venting. Electric models need amperage. Sizing is based on flow in gallons per minute and how far the incoming water has to be heated. Hot, humid summers and cool winters with frequent freeze and thaw cycles are the main climate stresses on plumbing in Tennessee. Isolation valves go on every unit for descaling.

    PEX Pipe Installation in Nashville, TN

    PEX bends around corners, shrugs off corrosion and needs far fewer fittings than copper. On a whole-house repipe that means fewer joints hidden behind drywall and fewer places for a slow leak to start rotting a stud. It can also stretch when water freezes. Before a freeze, homeowners here usually disconnect hoses, close crawlspace vents and let faucets drip on outside walls. Stretch or not, we still insulate runs in exterior walls and crawl spaces.

    Softener Loop and Conditioner Installs in Nashville

    Mineral buildup from harder water can wear out water heaters and leave scale on faucets, showerheads and dishwasher parts. Some homes already have a softener loop plumbed in, usually in the garage, which turns the install into a two-hour job. Without a loop, we tie into the main and build one. That adds a few hours. The same loop works for a salt softener or a salt-free conditioner.

    Sump Pump Installation in Nashville, TN

    A sump pump that fails during a storm usually failed months earlier: a stuck float, a seized impeller, a dead check valve or a cord unplugged for the shop vac. The pit goes to proper depth, the primary pump is sized for the inflow, and a battery backup is added on request. Where the water exits matters most. The discharge gets carried well away from the foundation so the same water is not pumped twice.
