    After-Hours Disposal Service in Nashville

    Preventive Care and Longevity in Nashville, TN

    Keep cold water running before, during and after use, and let it go about 15 seconds after the motor stops so the food clears the trap. Grease poured in with hot water coats the chamber and the pipe, while cold water keeps it solid so the impellers can break it up. Fibrous scraps jam it most. Throw out celery, corn husks and artichoke leaves.

    Emergency Disposal and Sink Backup Repair in Nashville

    A failed disposal can push water back into the sink and dishwasher. If water is rising or reaching the floor, shut off the disposal and the dishwasher and call us, since running either one just adds more water to the cabinet. We move quickly on these.

    Electrical Issues and Motor Repair in Nashville

    A disposal that hums but will not grind is getting power but is stuck, while one that is silent has no power at all. They are different problems. We check the breaker, the reset button and the switch for silent units, and the flywheel and motor windings for humming ones, then decide if a repair makes sense.

    Noisy Disposal Repair in Nashville

    Grinding, rattling or a hum that sounds rough all point to something inside. A loose mount will rattle. We check the mount, then look for debris, then run the motor empty, since a whine with an empty chamber means the bearings are going.

    Jam Clearing and Motor Repair in Nashville

    Bones, fruit pits and bottle caps lodge between the grind ring and the impeller lugs, and the motor stalls. We shut off power, insert a 1/4 inch hex key into the socket on the bottom of the unit and work it back and forth until the flywheel turns freely. Next we pull the object with pliers. Long stalls can overheat the motor, so we run it under load before we finish.

    Disposal Repair vs Replacement Costs in Nashville, TN

    Jams and minor leaks usually cost far less than a new unit. If the disposal is past eight to ten years old, leaks from the body or has a failing motor, replacement is usually the better value, and we give you both prices when it is close. You make the call.

    Jammed Disposal Repair in Nashville

    Jams usually trace back to one hard object, like a peach pit, a chicken bone or a twist tie wrapped around the impeller. We free the flywheel from below with the hex key and pull the object with pliers, never a hand, then run it with water to make sure nothing else is stuck. The reset button may need a press. Repeat jams point us to the trap arm.
