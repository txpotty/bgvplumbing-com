    Sewer Repair Options for Nashville, TN Homes

    Root Intrusion Repair in Nashville, TN

    Cutting roots treats the symptom. Fixing the joint treats the cause. After clearing the line, we look at each rooted joint on camera and decide between a short sectional liner, a full liner or cutting in new pipe, depending on how many joints are affected and how far apart they sit. One or two joints is a small job. A line rooted end to end is a lining or replacement job.

    Bellied Sewer Line Correction in Nashville, TN

    A belly is a low spot where the pipe has sagged and holds standing water. Paper and solids settle there. Karst ground with sinkholes is common in parts of Tennessee, and shifting soil above that rock can crack buried pipes over time. We measure the depth of standing water on camera, dig out that section, re-bed the pipe on compacted gravel and reset the slope to the quarter inch per foot most codes call for on residential laterals. A shallow belly sometimes just needs regular cleaning.

    Sewer Vent Pipe Repair in Nashville, TN

    A plugged vent makes drains gurgle and traps run dry. Then the sewer smell shows up. Bird nests, leaves and ice can block a roof vent, and older galvanized or cast iron vent stacks crack inside the walls where nobody sees them, which is why we look at the vent whenever the lateral checks out fine. Fixes range from a quick clearing to a new section of stack.

    Sewer Line Backups and Blockages in Nashville

    Water coming up in the tub when the washer drains is a classic main line symptom. The clog is past the branches. We clear it from the nearest cleanout, then run the camera to see what caused it, because wipes, grease and roots all call for different follow-up. Older Tennessee homes with clay lines often show roots right at the joints. We record what we find.

    Emergency Sewer Repair in Nashville

    Raw sewage on a basement floor is not a next-week problem. Our phone is answered after hours. The first goal on an emergency call is to get the line flowing, usually with a cable or jetter, and then camera it to find out whether you have a clog or a structural failure that will back up again tomorrow. Sometimes the permanent fix waits for daylight. Until then, we tell you which fixtures are safe to use.

    Restaurant and Retail Sewer Repair in Nashville

    Restaurants, retail centers and multifamily buildings load a lateral far beyond a single house. Grease is the usual culprit. We jet the line, camera it, and repair the weak section with options that limit how long you are closed, such as overnight work or temporary bypass pumps that keep the building running. A two-way cleanout usually goes in at the same time.

    Root Intrusion Removal in Nashville, TN

    Roots enter through hairline cracks and loose joints, then grow into a mat that catches paper and grease. Cut roots regrow within a year or two. We clear the line with a root cutting head or a jetter with a chain flail, camera it afterward to see the condition of each joint, and tell you whether sealing or replacing those joints makes more sense than cleaning every spring. Mature trees in Nashville yards make this a common call.

    Traditional Sewer Line Replacement in Nashville

    Some lines are too far gone for a liner. A fully collapsed section is one example. When the camera shows a collapse, a large offset or a belly deep enough to hold several inches of water, we open a trench, remove the old pipe and lay new Schedule 40 PVC on proper bedding at a quarter inch of fall per foot. We see this most in older neighborhoods with clay pipe. The dig is staged so the house is without a sewer for as little time as possible.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Sewer Repair in Nashville

    A single spot repair typically runs $1,500 to $4,500. Deeper pipe costs more to reach. Trenchless lining usually lands between $80 and $250 per foot, and full replacement of a typical residential lateral ranges from $5,000 to $15,000, depending on length and what sits on top of the pipe. We price it in writing after the video.

    For a spot repair, you lose the sewer for a few hours. Replacements take longer. On a full replacement we stage the work so the house is reconnected each evening where possible, and we tell you which fixtures are safe to use while the line is open. Moving out is rarely necessary.

    A spot repair or a jetting job usually takes a few hours. Pipe lining runs one to two days. A full replacement with excavation can take two to five days, with length, depth and the city inspection schedule setting the pace. We give you a timeline with the quote.

    A rental auger can open a soft clog. Structural damage stays. Forcing a cable through a broken or offset joint can snag, tear the pipe further or leave a cutter head stuck underground, and then someone has to dig to get the cable out. Repeat clogs deserve a video inspection.

    Clay, cast iron, ABS, PVC and Orangeburg all come up in Nashville and around Tennessee. We work on all five. Joining new PVC to old clay or cast iron takes a shielded transition coupling sized to the outside diameter of each pipe, because a mismatched coupling is the most common reason a spot repair fails. The usual couplings ride on the truck.

    We use Schedule 40 PVC or ABS that meets local code. Transitions get shielded couplings. The coupling has to match the outside diameter of both pipes, and for lining work we use epoxy-saturated liners rated for decades of service once they cure inside the old pipe. Everything we use is listed for underground sewer.

    Multiple drains backing up at once is the big one. The tub often shows it first. Water rising in the shower when the washer drains, a sewage smell near the cleanout, and wet or very green patches over the line tell you the trouble is in the main line, not one fixture. Video makes it certain.
