    Electric Element Check in Gallatin

    Electric tanks have two elements, usually 4,500 watts at 240 volts, and the lower one does most of the work and takes most of the scale. Each element gets an ohm reading. If the hot water quits halfway through a shower, the lower element is the first suspect. We replace it with the matching wattage and voltage.

    Attic and Garage Tank Checkups in Gallatin, TN

    Garage and attic tanks live through bigger temperature swings than closet units, which wears out plastic drain valves and wiring insulation faster. Attic tanks need a working pan. We confirm the pan drain line is open and runs to a safe spot. Gas tanks in garages need their ignition source at least 18 inches off the floor unless they are FVIR models.

    Anode Rod Inspection and Replacement in Gallatin, TN

    The anode rod is a sacrificial metal rod that corrodes so the steel tank does not.     Gas and Electric Tank Checks in Gallatin, TN

    Gas and electric tanks share the same steel shell, but the parts that wear out are different. On gas units we inspect the burner, thermocouple and vent connector. On electric units we meter both elements and check each thermostat terminal for scorching. Both get a flush.

    Rental and Commercial Tank Logs in Gallatin

    Landlords and property managers with a dozen units need to know which tank is next to fail. We log every tank we service.     Burner and Pilot Service in Gallatin, TN

    On a gas tank, a dirty burner throws a lazy yellow flame and leaves soot on the flue baffle. The burner comes out and gets brushed and vacuumed. A tired thermocouple drops the pilot, so we test it too. The goal is a steady blue flame.

    Relief Valve Testing in Gallatin

    Temperature and pressure relief valves on residential tanks are typically rated to open at 150 psi or 210 degrees, and they can stick shut after years of mineral buildup. We lift the test lever and watch it reseat. A valve that drips afterward gets replaced. We also confirm the discharge pipe runs full size to within six inches of the floor or to an approved drain, which is what plumbing codes ask for.
