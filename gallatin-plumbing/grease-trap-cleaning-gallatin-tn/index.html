    Compliance and Documentation in Gallatin

    Pretreatment rules in Tennessee are usually set and enforced at the city or sewer district level, and nearly all of them want proof of pumping kept on site. We leave a dated record every visit.     Grease Trap Inspection and Repair in Gallatin

    An empty trap is the only time anyone can see what is wrong with it. We look for cracked baffles and missing outlet tees. A missing outlet tee lets the floating grease cap pour straight into the sewer, and it is one of the most common failures we find on older concrete interceptors. We can often fix small problems on the spot.

    Indoor Grease Trap Cleaning in Gallatin

    Indoor traps usually sit under the three-compartment sink or in a recessed pit near the dish station. Room to work is tight. Mats go down first, the contents get pumped or scooped into sealed containers, and the floor gets wiped so prep can start right after. Gasket and flow control get checked too.

    Scheduled Grease Trap Maintenance Plans in Gallatin

    Most pretreatment programs use the 25 percent rule: pump before floating grease and settled solids together take up a quarter of the trap's liquid depth. Other programs simply set a fixed interval, commonly 90 days for large interceptors. Whichever rule your program uses, we schedule to it. Changes are easy.

    Outdoor Grease Interceptor Pumping in Gallatin, TN

    In-ground interceptors need a vacuum truck and usually a pressure wand to break up the cap. We open every compartment and pump each one to the floor. With the tank empty, we inspect the tees, baffle wall, risers and lid seals, since a cracked riser lets dirt and groundwater into the tank. Lids go back on tight.

    Large Outdoor Grease Interceptor Cleaning in Gallatin

    Gravity interceptors in the parking lot often hold 750 to 2,000 gallons across two or three compartments. We pump each compartment on its own. A hard grease cap gets broken up with a pressure wand before pumping, otherwise it plugs the suction hose and half of it stays in the tank. No one goes inside without confined-space gear.
