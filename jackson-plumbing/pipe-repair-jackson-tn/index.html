    Galvanized Pipe Repair in Jackson

    Threaded steel pipe rusts from the inside until flow drops and threaded joints start to leak. Repairs are possible. The trouble is that every fitting you disturb on old galvanized can start a new leak, so we replace failing sections with PEX or copper using dielectric fittings and talk through a full replacement plan when the rest is close behind. You decide the timing.

    Frozen and Burst Line Repair in Jackson

    Pipes in exterior walls, attics and crawlspaces freeze first. Ice splits the pipe. The split usually does not leak until the ice thaws, which is why many burst pipes are found hours after the cold snap. We repair the split and add insulation. Heat tape is an option on problem runs.

    PEX and Copper Pipe Repair in Jackson, TN

    PEX and copper fail in different ways. Copper pins. Copper thins from the inside and develops pinholes, while PEX usually fails at fittings or where it was kinked during installation, so the truck carries tools and fittings for both. Each repair is pressure tested.

    PEX Pipe Repair and Installation in Jackson

    PEX is flexible and handles freezing better than rigid pipe. It still fails. Most PEX leaks happen at a crimp ring that was not fully seated, an early brass fitting that dezincified or a section damaged by sunlight in an attic, and we cut out the bad fitting and replace it with a crimp, clamp or expansion connection rated for the pipe. Most take under an hour.

    Slab Leak Pipe Repair in Jackson, TN

    Slab leaks start when pipe under the concrete wears through or gets stressed by soil movement. Crawlspaces are the most common foundation here, though basements are more frequent in hilly areas where homes are built into slopes. A hot spot underfoot is common. We isolate the leaking line with pressure tests and find it with acoustic and thermal equipment, and you pick between a targeted floor repair and a reroute overhead. We quote each way.

    Leaky Pipe Repair in Jackson, TN

    Small leaks raise water bills and grow mold in hidden spaces. The meter tells you. If the dial moves with every fixture off, there is a leak somewhere, so we shut off branches one at a time to find which part of the system is losing water. Then we open one spot.

    Corroded Pipe Replacement in Jackson

    Galvanized steel and old copper both corrode with age. Rusty water is a sign. Low pressure, discolored water and leaks at threaded joints tell us the pipe is closing off from the inside, so we swap the failing pipe for PEX or copper with proper fittings between metals. Pressure comes right back.
