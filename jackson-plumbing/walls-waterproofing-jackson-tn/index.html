    Foundation Crack Repair in Jackson

    Crack injection takes about two hours per crack.     Frequently Asked Questions About Walls Waterproofing in Jackson

    Shower wall waterproofing with a membrane system usually runs $1,200 to $3,500 including demolition and backer board, with the new tile priced separately. Block wall sealing outside often costs $800 to $3,000. Foundation waterproofing below grade is usually $2,500 to $8,000.

    Waterproofing takes away the moisture mold needs to grow. Existing mold should be removed and the area dried before any sealer goes on, and for larger growth, roughly anything over ten square feet, we recommend a remediation company. We remove smaller affected areas ourselves.

    Simple crack injection usually does not need one. Excavation, structural repairs and changes to drainage or the sump discharge often do, and your local building department decides which jobs need one, so we check before we quote and handle the application when a permit is required. We list any permit fee on the quote.

    It depends on the wall and the site. On exterior foundation work we use rubberized asphalt sheet membranes, polyurethane-modified asphalt liquids, bentonite panels and crystalline coatings, picking the one that suits the soil and the wall material. For showers, we use Kerdi, RedGard or Hydro Ban.

    Xypex, Tremco, Sika and Henry products show up on a lot of our jobs, from crystalline admixtures to elastomeric membranes and sealants. The product depends on the wall. Poured concrete takes crystalline coatings well, while block often needs an elastomeric membrane or a drainage board.

    Interior systems manage water after it has come through the wall, with drain tile along the footing and a sump to pump it out. Exterior waterproofing stops water before it reaches the wall, which means digging down to the footing and applying a membrane to the outside face. Outside costs more.

    Small crack patches, sure. A complete system takes knowledge of drainage, foundation behavior and the application steps for each product, and mistakes are expensive since they stay hidden behind finished walls for years. Most DIY failures we see started at the footing joint.

    Patching one small crack yourself is fine. A full system involves excavation, digging equipment, proper membrane thickness and knowing how water drains around a footing, plus local code on drainage and backfill. A bad install can trap water against the wall and make things worse.
