    Electric Tankless Water Heater Installation in Jackson, TN

    Electric tankless heaters make sense in Jackson, TN homes without gas, or for a single sink or a guest bath far from the main heater. Whole-house electric units pull a lot of current. Some need two or three 40 to 60 amp double-pole breakers, and a 100-amp panel often cannot carry that on top of the air conditioner and range. We check the panel first.

    Commercial Tankless Water Heater Installation in Jackson

    Commercial hot water loads in Jackson are heavier and more constant than residential. A busy restaurant may run a dish machine, three hand sinks and a mop sink at once, and the tankless rack has to hold temperature through the whole lunch rush without dropping a degree. We rack units with a common manifold. Service one, keep the others going.

    Tank to Tankless Conversions in Jackson

    Conversions in Jackson tend to run a full workday. The old tank gets drained and hauled off, the gas line is upsized and tested, a new vent goes out through a wall or roof, and the unit is mounted, plumbed with isolation valves and fired. Hot water is back by evening. The inspector comes after.

    Upgrading from Tank to Tankless in Jackson, TN

    Switching from a tank takes more than unbolting one and hanging the other. A 40-gallon tank burns about 40,000 BTU, but a whole-home tankless unit may need 150,000 to 199,000, so the meter line nearly always gets bigger. The vent changes as well. Both are on the estimate.

    Residential Tankless Water Heater Installation in Jackson

    Sizing a home unit comes down to two numbers: peak gallons per minute and temperature rise. A shower head flows about 2 gallons per minute and a kitchen faucet a bit less, so a house that runs two showers and a sink together needs roughly 5 to 6 gallons at the rise your inlet water calls for. It shortens the list quickly.

    Rinnai, Navien and Noritz Installation in Jackson, TN

    We install Rinnai, Navien, Noritz and Rheem in Jackson, TN homes all year long. Warranty terms vary. We explain the differences.

    Tankless Water Heater Relocation in Jackson

    Moving the heater is common on a tankless job because the old tank spot is not always the best place. Mounting near the gas meter on a garage or outside wall shortens the gas line and venting, though it can add distance to the bathrooms. Both distances matter. Long hot runs may call for recirculation.

    Hard Water Protection for Tankless Units in Jackson, TN

    Calcium scale is the most common reason tankless heaters fail. Water hardness in Tennessee varies by source, and areas that draw from limestone aquifers or springs tend to have fairly hard water. Scale coats the heat exchanger, cuts efficiency and triggers error codes, and left alone it can crack the exchanger and end the warranty claim before it starts. A yearly flush prevents it. Service valves make it easy.
