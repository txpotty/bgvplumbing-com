    Copper Pinhole Leak Repair in Jackson

    Pinhole leaks are tiny holes that form when the inner wall of copper pipe erodes or pits. They often appear in thin Type M pipe and on hot water lines where velocity and temperature are higher. We cut out the affected section and replace it with new Type L copper or a PEX transition. While the wall is open, we inspect nearby pipe for discoloration or thinning.

    Corrosion-Related Leak Repair in Jackson

    Corrosion is a natural enemy of copper pipes and is made worse by certain water chemistries. Water hardness in Tennessee varies by source, and areas that draw from limestone aquifers or springs tend to have fairly hard water. This can lead to thinning pipe walls and eventual leaks. Our team assesses the extent of corrosion, which may show up as blue-green stains on pipes or fixtures. For localized corrosion, we perform spot repairs, but if corrosion is widespread, we may recommend re-piping sections to prevent recurring issues. We make sure all new connections are properly sealed and protected.

    Accessible copper leak repairs typically cost $250 to $650. Repairs inside finished walls or ceilings usually range from $500 to $1,500 before drywall patching. Slab leak repairs on copper lines frequently run $1,500 to $4,000.

    While temporary fixes like clamps or epoxy putties exist, professional repair is the better choice for lasting results. DIY repairs often fail, leading to more significant water damage and costly secondary issues.     Type K, L and M copper pipes differ primarily in their wall thickness and thus their pressure ratings. Type K has the thickest walls and is used for underground service and main water lines. Type L is common for interior water supply and radiant heating. Type M has the thinnest walls and is usually used for less demanding applications. We are skilled in repairing all types.

    Most accessible repairs take one to two hours. Leaks inside walls or ceilings can take two to four hours, and slab leak repairs may take a whole day.

    We usually use Type L copper, which has thicker walls than Type M and lasts longer. In some situations we use ProPress fittings or a PEX transition.

    Copper pipe leaks are often caused by corrosion, especially from hard or acidic water. Water hardness in Tennessee varies by source, and areas that draw from limestone aquifers or springs tend to have moderately hard water. Other causes include excessive water pressure, improper installation, shifting foundations putting stress on joints and freezing temperatures that can cause pipes to burst. Pinhole leaks are a common symptom of internal corrosion.

    Common causes include aggressive water chemistry, high water velocity, excessive pressure, poor original soldering and stray electrical current from grounding issues. These factors erode the pipe wall from the inside over the years.
